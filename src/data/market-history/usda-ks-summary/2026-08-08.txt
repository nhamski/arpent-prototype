Compared to last week, steers sold 7.00 to 15.00 higher. Heifers sold steady to 5.00 higher. Demand was good. No recent price comparison
on slaughter cows and bulls so no trend available. Compared to last week, trading of Kansas negotiated fed cattle sold 2.00 higher at 235.00.
In the hay market, hay movement and prices remained steady across the state. Much of Kansas continues to need significant moisture to
support alfalfa production and row crop development. Third cutting alfalfa is generally yielding below average in most regions, with many
producers reporting only marginal production. Prairie hay harvest is progressing, with overall yields reported as average. Supply included:
88% Feeder Cattle (77% Steers, 23% Heifers, 0% Bulls); 9% Slaughter Cattle (90% Cows, 10% Bulls, 0% Dairy Bulls); 2% Replacement Cattle
(14% Stock Cows, 53% Bred Cows, 2% Bred Heifers, 31% Cow-Calf Pairs). Feeder cattle supply over 600 lbs was 94%.
