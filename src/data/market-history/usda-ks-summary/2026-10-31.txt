Compared to last week, steers under 600 lbs sold steady to 10.00 higher; 600-850 lbs 4.00 to 12.00 lower; over 850 lbs steady to 3.00 higher.
Heifers under 650 lbs sold 10.00 to 15.00 higher; 650-850 lbs steady to 5.00 higher; over 850 lbs 2.00 to 5.00 lower. Demand was moderate
to good. Slaughter cows and bulls sold unevenly steady. Negotiated cash fed cattle sold at 225.00 in the South Central area this week. In the
hay market, demand remained steady last week, while movement was slightly lower due to weather conditions and limited truck availability.
Drying conditions allowed fieldwork to resume, with producers continuing to plant winter wheat, swath alfalfa, and harvest soybeans. A light
freeze occurred across much of the state but was not severe enough to cause significant crop damage. Supply included: 84% Feeder Cattle
(49% Steers, 46% Heifers, 1% Bulls, 3% Beef/Dairy Steers, 1% Beef/Dairy Heifers); 8% Slaughter Cattle (95% Cows, 5% Bulls); 9% Replacement
Cattle (9% Stock Cows, 73% Bred Cows, 7% Bred Heifers, 10% Open Heifers, 1% Cow-Calf Pairs). Feeder cattle supply over 600 lbs was 66%.
