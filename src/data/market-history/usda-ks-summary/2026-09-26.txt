Compared to last week, steers sold uneven; 2.00 lower to 4.00 higher Heifers 500-700 lbs sold 10.00 to 20.00 higher on a much better test
than last weeks trading in the weight range. Other weights of heifers sold steady to 5.00 lower. Demand was moderate to good. Slaughter
cows sold steady to 3.00 lower; with slaughter bulls selling steady to 3.00 higher. for the 3 auction markets representing this weeks supply.
In the hay market, demand remained steady while movement slowed as available hay inventories began to diminish. As drought conditions
worsen and producers explore alternative feed sources, producers should be aware of pesticide label requirements regarding what can and
cannot be used for livestock feed. According to the US Drought Monitor for the week of September 17th, Most of the High Plains received
beneficial rainfall, resulting in improvement to drought categories over parts of all states in the region. More precipitation will be needed,
however, to ameliorate the continued extreme drought over much of Colorado and Wyoming as well as parts of Montana, North and South
Dakota, and western Nebraska. Areas of exceptional drought are still shown in much of central Colorado and western Nebraska. Supply
included: 90% Feeder Cattle (59% Steers, 40% Heifers, 1% Bulls); 5% Slaughter Cattle (92% Cows, 8% Bulls); 4% Replacement Cattle (8% Stock
Cows, 77% Bred Cows, 12% Bred Heifers, 3% Cow-Calf Pairs). Feeder cattle supply over 600 lbs was 71%.
