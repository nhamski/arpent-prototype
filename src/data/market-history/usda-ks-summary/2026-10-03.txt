Compared to last week, steers sold uneven; 2.00 lower to 2.00 higher. Heifers sold steady to 7.00 lower. Demand was moderate. Slaughter
cows and bulls sold 1.00 to 4.00 lower. In the hay market, demand remained steady while movement slowed as available hay inventories
began to diminish. As drought conditions worsen and producers explore alternative feed sources, producers should be aware of pesticide
label requirements regarding what can and cannot be used for livestock feed. According to the US Drought Monitor for the week of
September 24th, The High Plains states experienced almost exclusively drought improvements this week, as many areas experienced
beneficial rainfall, with Colorado, Kansas, and Nebraska being beneficiaries of the east-Pacific El Nino tropical connection. Despite
temperatures being up to 6 or more degrees above average, the plentiful rainfall has contributed to drought improvement in many areas this
week. Supply included: 90% Feeder Cattle (53% Steers, 47% Heifers, 0% Bulls); 6% Slaughter Cattle (95% Cows, 5% Bulls); 4% Replacement
Cattle (23% Stock Cows, 72% Bred Cows, 4% Bred Heifers, 1% Cow-Calf Pairs). Feeder cattle supply over 600 lbs was 74%.
